The frontier

Questions worth exploring.

Every department can approach its field from several angles. These focus areas are a starting point for research, design, and practical learning.

FOCUS / 01

Astronomy

Use careful sky observations and reliable references to understand objects beyond Earth.

FOCUS / 02

Orbital mechanics

Explore how gravity, speed, and trajectories shape the motion of spacecraft and planets.

FOCUS / 03

Space technology

Investigate the systems, constraints, and design trade-offs behind missions and payloads.

Workbench / 03

From ideas to evidence.

Explore the kinds of notes, design studies, and experiments that belong in this department. Each work card is an exploration track; add dated photos, methods, results, and lessons as real work becomes ready to share.

OBSERVATION TRACK

Night-sky log

Document observation dates, sky conditions, visible objects, and reference sources so notes can be understood and checked later.

Observations · Source notes
DESIGN STUDY

Orbits on paper

Use diagrams and introductory calculations to explore orbital paths, transfer concepts, and the assumptions behind a simplified model.

Models · Calculations
RESEARCH TRACK

Space systems sketchbook

Break a spacecraft or payload into subsystems, map what each needs to do, and identify the engineering questions still open.

System maps · Design questions
A transparent lab notebook beats a big claim. Document what was tested, what was observed, what remains uncertain, and what should happen next.

Our shared method

Keep the learning visible.

01 / ASK

Start with a question

Define what you want to understand, and why the question matters.

02 / EXPLORE

Test and document

Choose a suitable method, keep useful notes, and be honest about limits.

03 / SHARE

Explain the result

Show the evidence, explain what changed, and identify the next question.
